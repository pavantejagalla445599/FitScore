🏃 FitScore — Gamified Wellness & Insurance Rewards

ML-driven activity scoring, behavioural personas, and streak-risk nudges, feeding a transparent insurer-style reward policy.

The problem

Insurers increasingly want to reward customers for healthy behaviour with premium discounts — it lowers long-term risk and keeps customers engaged. But they need a fair, transparent, data-driven way to decide who earns what. Guesswork or a single raw number isn't defensible.

What the insurer gets

• Personalised, explainable rewards — every discount comes with a plain-English reason.
• Early churn detection — the model flags customers likely to disengage before they lapse.
• Fairness by design — ML provides the intelligence; a transparent policy makes the decision.

Reward-discount percentages are illustrative demonstration assumptions — the dataset has no real premiums or claims, so FitScore provides behavioural intelligence, not actuarial pricing.

📐 Business rules, weights & assumptions

Everything the app computes traces back to one of the rules below — nothing is hidden inside a model.

Wellness score (0–90, rule-based — not ML)
steps_points = min(steps / 10,000 × 40, 40)
active_points = min(very_active×1.0 + fairly_active×0.6 + lightly_active×0.2, 30)
sleep_points = 20 if sleep is 420–540 min (7–9h), else min(sleep/480 × 15, 15)
daily_wellness_score = steps_points + active_points + sleep_points   (max 90)
Reward score (0–100, business policy — combines ML outputs, is NOT itself ML)
reward_score = 0.50 × wellness_component
    + 0.20 × consistency_component
    + 0.15 × streak_safety_component  (= (1 − risk_probability) × 100, from the classifier)
    + 0.15 × forecast_component  (= min(forecast_steps / 10,000 × 100, 100), from Linear Regression)

Streak-risk label definition
A day is labelled at-risk if next-day steps < 0.7 × (trailing 3-day average steps). This is the ground-truth label the 4 classifiers are trained to predict — it is a rule, not something the model invented.
Assumptions & limitations
The reward tiers are an illustrative demonstration policy, not a real actuarial premium calculation — this data has no insurance claims, premiums, or medical-cost information in it.
K-Means persona labels (e.g. "Weekend Warrior") are assigned by step-count rank order; the clustering doesn't independently verify that a persona's behaviour matches its name.
Consistency on the Predict New User tab is self-reported, since a brand-new user has no day-by-day history yet to compute it from.
Model selection uses F1-score on the at-risk class rather than raw accuracy, because the at-risk label is imbalanced — see the Streak Risk tab for the full comparison.
